PROJECT PROPOSAL & DATABASE DESIGN
Project Title
Campus Auditorium & Lecture Hall Reservation and Administrative Authorization System (CampusBook)

1. Introduction and Background
Universities and educational institutions host hundreds of academic and extracurricular gatherings every semester, ranging from combined department lectures, guest seminars, and examinations to student club hackathons, technical symposiums, and cultural festivals. These events rely heavily on finite campus venue infrastructure, primarily:
1. Central Auditoriums and Mini-Auditoriums (large seating capacity, specialized audio-visual consoles, stage lighting, and acoustic setups).
2. Smart Lecture Halls and Seminar Halls (medium capacity, smart podiums, projection arrays, and tiered seating).

Managing auditorium and lecture hall bookings is inherently complex and cannot be treated as a trivial first-come-first-served calendar booking system. High-demand campus venues require rigorous administrative oversight:
● Authorization & Credentials: Student club heads must furnish verified club registration, faculty coordinator approvals, and anticipated participant metrics. Faculty members require department validation for large multi-section lecture merges, symposia, or guest lectures.
● Administrative Vetting: An Administrator (e.g., Estate Officer, Dean of Student Affairs, or Academic Facilities Manager) must oversee all venues, review pending requests, verify the legitimacy of requesters and their authorizations, verify that the slot is completely vacant, and explicitly grant a formal permit.
● Temporal Integrity & Concurrency: Multiple faculty coordinators or club heads frequently apply for the exact same premium slots (e.g., Friday afternoons or weekends). Without atomic, database-level conflict prevention, concurrent booking submissions and parallel administrator approvals lead to devastating double-booking anomalies and scheduling clashes.

The proposed system, CampusBook, provides a unified, relational database-driven platform designed specifically to manage auditorium and lecture hall allocation, enforce authorization hierarchies, handle high-concurrency requests, and prevent slot clashes at the database engine level.

2. Problem Statement
Manual, spreadsheet-based, or naive reservation systems suffer from critical structural shortcomings in university environments:
● Double Booking & Slot Overlaps: Standard unique constraints (such as UNIQUE(venue_id, start_time)) fail to prevent overlapping ranges (e.g., Booking A from 10:00 to 13:00 conflicts with Booking B from 11:30 to 14:00).
● Race Conditions During Approval: If two administrators simultaneously approve two competing requests for the same hall, or if an approval races with a new reservation, naive application-level checks allow race conditions to bypass validation.
● Unverified & Unauthorized Access: Club heads or individuals may attempt to book large-capacity auditoriums without required institutional clearance, faculty endorsements, or valid club credentials.
● Lack of Centralized Administrative Oversight: Administrators lack a unified dashboard to review slot conflicts, inspect competing candidate applications for the same time window, review equipment/capacity requirements, and issue official digital permits.
● No Auditability & Utilization Intelligence: University leadership cannot reliably audit who authorized a venue, track cancellation histories, or assess resource utilization rates across academic departments and student bodies.

The proposed system solves this problem by maintaining an ACID-compliant PostgreSQL relational database that manages users, roles, venues, clubs, events, credentials, bookings, administrative approvals, and concurrency control in an integrated, mathematically verifiable manner.

3. Objectives
The main objectives of the proposed system are:
1. To provide a centralized relational system for booking campus auditoriums and lecture halls.
2. To establish a clear two-tier user access architecture:
   - Normal Users: Faculty (academic lectures, exams, symposia) and Student Club Heads (workshops, club events).
   - Administrators: Estate/Facilities Administrators who oversee all halls and grant permits upon authorization verification.
3. To enforce prerequisite authorization and credential checks before a venue booking can be submitted or approved (using relational division).
4. To prevent slot clashes and double bookings at the database engine level using PostgreSQL GiST range exclusions (tstzrange with btree_gist).
5. To eliminate concurrency race conditions during booking creation and administrative approvals using pessimistic row locks (SELECT ... FOR UPDATE) and atomic database transactions.
6. To automate conflict resolution so that approving one booking atomically flags or rejects competing pending requests for the same slot.
7. To maintain complete booking, approval, and audit history for institutional accountability.
8. To generate analytical reports on venue utilization, peak-hour bottlenecks, club activity, and approval turnaround times.

6. Database Selection and Justification
6.1 Selected Database
PostgreSQL 16+

6.2 Database Type
Relational Database Management System (RDBMS)

6.3 Justification
PostgreSQL is chosen because venue scheduling and administrative authorization demand absolute ACID integrity and specialized temporal mechanics:
1. PostgreSQL GiST Range Exclusion:
   Standard databases only enforce equality constraints (UNIQUE(venue_id, start_datetime)). This cannot prevent interval overlaps. PostgreSQL provides native range types (tstzrange) and GiST exclusion constraints:
   
   ALTER TABLE bookings ADD CONSTRAINT no_overlapping_confirmed_bookings
   EXCLUDE USING gist (
       venue_id WITH =,
       tstzrange(start_datetime, end_datetime, '[)') WITH &&
   )
   WHERE (booking_status = 'CONFIRMED');

   This guarantees that no two confirmed bookings for the same venue can overlap in time, enforced mathematically at the database engine level.
2. ACID Concurrency & Row-Level Locking:
   Using SELECT ... FOR UPDATE within stored procedures prevents race conditions when multiple administrators review or approve requests simultaneously.
3. Relational Division:
   PostgreSQL handles relational division queries efficiently via set difference (EXCEPT) or aggregate filters to ensure requesters meet all venue prerequisites.
4. Extensible Stored Procedures (PL/pgSQL):
   Business rules for atomic approval, slot validation, and cascading conflict rejection are implemented directly inside database transactions.
